How we did it

A cloth, a camera
and a shoe.

01

Wipe

On every court we wiped 2 or 3 same-size patches with a clean white cloth, same strokes every time.

02

Photograph

Our code compares each dirty cloth with a clean one and measures how much darker it got.

03

Pull

We weighted a shoe with a water bottle and dragged it with a spring scale. The less force it takes to slide, the slipperier the floor.

1

Where

Texas A&M Rec Center: __GROUPS__ court types, __COURTS__ courts, __SAMPLES__ cloth samples in total (2 or 3 per court).

2

When

We measured at 4 PM. Staff clean around noon and 7 PM (badminton isn't cleaned in the evening), so we projected 7 PM and 10 PM from our 4 PM readings and that schedule.

3

The numbers

We put a full water bottle in the shoe so it weighed __SHOE__ lb in total, then pulled it sideways with a spring scale and read the force when it started to slide. On a clean floor that took __CLEAN__ lb. Dust % is how much less force each court needed compared with that clean floor.

4

Limits

Only 2–3 cloth samples per court (we averaged them), phone photos under gym lighting, and a homemade spring-scale pull test. Good for comparing courts, not lab-grade.

Try it yourself

Grocery list

  • White washcloths 2–3 per court + one clean one
  • Basketball shoe same shoe for every pull
  • Full water bottle goes in the shoe for weight
  • Spring scale read the force when it slides
  • Phone camera same lighting for every cloth
  • Spreadsheet court, time, pull force
  • Python + OpenCV photos → discoloration index
  • Rec staff's OK test between games, never during
